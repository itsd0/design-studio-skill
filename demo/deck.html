Ferry · local-first file transfer

Your files cross the room, not the internet.

A five-minute look at how Ferry moves data between your own machines.

The round trip

Everyday file sharing takes two hops where one would do.

Upload first

Files travel to a data center before they travel to the room next door.

Kept in the middle

Cloud copies outlive the transfer, and the sender can rarely say where they landed.

Metered

Storage and egress are billed per byte. The gigabit network in your room is not.

The fix

Direct transfer is a routing problem, not a storage problem.

If two devices share a network, the fastest path between them is the one you already own.

How it works

Four steps, none of them a server.

01

Pair

Two devices exchange a short code and stay bonded until you revoke them.

02

Pick

Drag files, pipe stdin, or name a path. Ferry queues them where they are.

03

Stream

Bytes move directly over the network, resumable at the exact byte.

04

Verify

Both ends hash what landed. The transfer is done when the hashes agree.

The usual route

Four apps and a cloud account for files that sat in the next room the whole time.

With Ferry

One command, both ends awake.

Streaming starts before the prompt returns, and nothing stays behind in the middle.
